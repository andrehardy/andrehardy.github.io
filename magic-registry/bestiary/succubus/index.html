The Account

The Succubus is the night-visitor of the old books, the mare that rides the sleeper, which comes in the small hours and sits upon the chest so that the sleeper wakes and cannot move.

A bodiless pressure of the Second Tier, of low sound and heavy air, which wakes the mind of a sleeper while the body remains held in sleep; the figure seen is the mind's own image of the weight.

S

Snorri Sturluson tells in the Ynglinga saga of Vanlandi, king of the Swedes, who stayed a winter in Finland, married there, and left, promising to return. He did not return. His wife paid a witch to draw him back or kill him, and at Uppsala, as he lay down to sleep, he cried out that the mara was treading on him. His men held his head, and she trod on his legs. When they held his legs, she pressed his head, and he died of it.

Of its kinds the tellers name many: the mara of the North, whose name is still in the English nightmare; the incubus and succubus of the churchmen, who came to sleepers in the shape most likely to undo them; the Old Hag of the outports of Newfoundland. Henry Fuseli painted it in 1781 as a squat demon on the breast of a sleeping woman, and the picture has frightened people since. The Registry counts them all as one.

It is found in bedrooms, in the hours after midnight, and with sleepers who lie on their backs. David Hufford, who went to Newfoundland in the 1970s to collect the Old Hag stories, found that the experience is the same in every place, whatever the tellers believe. The sleeper wakes and cannot move. Something is in the room, and something is pressing.

Of its body this much is certain: it has none, and the sleeper supplies the shape. The weight is real, the breathlessness is real and the terror is real, but the hag or the demon or the beautiful stranger is a face put on a pressure that has none.

It is not to be fought, for there is nothing to strike. The old remedy is to move a single finger, or a toe, and once anything moves, the weight is gone.

Body: none. What is perceived (a figure on the chest, a hag, a beautiful stranger, a shadow at the door) is supplied by the sleeper's own mind. Source: the Second Tier, as a pressure of very low sound and dense air rising from below. Mechanism: during dreaming sleep, when the body's muscles are held slack, the pressure wakes the mind without releasing the body. The sleeper is conscious, unable to move, short of breath, and certain of a presence. Duration: seconds to minutes. Trigger: lying on the back; broken sleep; the hours after midnight. Containment: none; the episode ends of itself, or with any small movement. Hazard to the observer: terror, without lasting harm.

Under the Screen

The weight

It is felt before it is seen, as a weight on the chest like a sitting body, and the breath grows short beneath it.

Pressure on the chest; laboured breathing.

The figure

The figure is never the same twice. To one sleeper it is an old woman, to another a beautiful stranger, to a third only a darkness at the foot of the bed.

The perceived form varies by observer and is supplied by the sleeper's mind.

The trace

On the drum the breath runs in long even waves until it comes, and then it goes flat and shallow, and the stylus barely moves.

Respiration amplitude falls sharply during an episode.

Its Habits

It comes to the still. After a few moments without movement the trace goes flat and the dark closes in from the edges of the room. The smallest movement wakes the sleeper, and the weight lifts. The page itself is never held.After five seconds of no input the trace flattens and a vignette closes. Any input ends the episode. Reading and movement through the page are never restricted.

In the reader's own deep night, from eleven until four, the dark closes further about the bed. It comes no sooner for that.From 23:00 to 04:00 local time the vignette closes deeper. The onset time is unchanged.

Any movement at all is enough to wake.Any key wakes it; by touch, any contact. With reduced motion a still trace is shown and no vignette closes.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Wraith. The Wraith also comes at night and also rises from below, but it is a room remembering, and no one is pressed by it. The Succubus presses on the living.Both are bodiless and of the Second Tier. The Wraith is a place's recording; the Succubus acts on a living sleeper.
