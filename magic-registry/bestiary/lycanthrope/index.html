The Account

The Lycanthrope is a man by day and a wolf by the full moon, and the change is no spell he casts but a weight he carries, which the moon draws up through his bones.

O

Of the wolf-man there are as many accounts as there are moors. Ovid tells of Lycaon, a king of Arcadia, who served human flesh to Jupiter and was turned into a wolf for it, keeping his grey hair and his savage look. The Romans told of a soldier who stripped by the tombs on a bright night, made water around his clothes, and ran off howling; the clothes had turned to stone. The Bretons call him bisclavret, the Normans garwaf, and the shepherds of every country between have a name for him and a story to go with it.

Of his kinds the Registry counts one, though it shows two faces. By day and through most of the month he is a man, and passes for one, though he runs hot, sleeps badly, and is restless when the moon fattens. At the full he is something else: taller, heavier, long in the jaw, and without the man’s mind to govern him.

He is found where people are few: on moors, at the forest’s edge, in the hill country. He is solitary by necessity, for a man who is a wolf one night in thirty soon learns to live where he will not be missed.

Of his body this much is certain: the wolf is not put on him from outside. It is in him already, folded inside his bones like a second skeleton, and the moon draws it out as it draws the sea. The change is not a melting but a breaking. The long bones crack and lengthen, the sinews tear and catch again, and the face is pulled forward into a muzzle. Those who have watched it say it is the sound they remember.

He is not to be pitied without care, nor feared without reason. The man may be gentle; the wolf is not anything at all but hunger. The old remedies are practical ones. Keep him from the full moon behind a strong door, and keep his clothes, for in the oldest tales the man returns to his shape only by them.

Body: human for most of the lunar month, with a dormant second form in the bone and connective tissue. Source: the Second Tier, inherited. Trigger: the full moon, when the illuminated fraction passes about 0.97; extreme strain (a fall, a fight, a sudden run) can start a partial change at any phase. Mechanism: the skeleton remodels in minutes. Bone thickens and lengthens, ligaments tear and re-anchor, the skull draws out into a muzzle. The change is not chosen and is not reversible until the moon wanes. Containment: restraint through the night; old accounts keep the clothes, since the return to human form goes by them. Hazard to the observer: severe, during the change and for the night that follows.
