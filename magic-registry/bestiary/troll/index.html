The Account

The Troll is a great slow giant of the mountains that walks only at night, for the first ray of the sun turns it to stone where it stands.

A Chimeric giant whose body is built for the pressure and dark of the deep earth, and in which direct sunlight sets the tissue rigid, as stone.

T

The farmers of Norway knew the trolls as their nearest and least welcome neighbours. In the tales that Peter Christen Asbjørnsen and Jørgen Moe gathered from the valleys in the 1840s, trolls live in the mountains, count their gold, keep stolen princesses, and are very large and very stupid. A troll can be beaten by a boy with his wits about him. More often it is beaten by keeping it talking until the sun comes up, for then it bursts or turns to stone.

Of their kinds there are many: the mountain troll, the forest troll, the troll-wife with her long nose, the troll king of the Dovre, whose daughter Ibsen’s Peer Gynt went into the mountain to woo. The Registry counts them as one, for every account agrees on the sun.

They are found on the high fjells, in the birch woods below them, and under the bridges in the valleys. Stones of strange shape are pointed out up and down the country as trolls caught out at dawn, and the people of each district can say which troll each one was.

Of their bodies this much is certain: they are built for the deep. They are huge, heavy and slow, with skin like weathered rock and eyes that cannot bear the light. Under the sun the body hardens from the skin inward and stops. Three trolls in Tolkien’s book were caught arguing over how to cook their supper. The dawn caught them, and they stand there still, by the road in the Trollshaws.

They are not to be met at night, or answered when they call, or let in. By day they are only stones, and a traveller may sit in their shade.

Body: very large, heavy-boned, with a hide of mineral density and slow circulation. Source: the Second Tier, as structure: the body is adapted to depth, weight and darkness. Mechanism: under direct sunlight the tissue mineralizes from the surface inward within moments, and the body stops as stone. Folk accounts disagree on whether this is permanent; the Registry records that it lasts at least until the sun has set. Signs: boulders of human-like shape in odd places; tracks that end at a stone. Behaviour: solitary, territorial, slow to anger, and slow to stop. Containment: daylight. Hazard to the observer: grave at night, nil at noon.

Under the Screen

The hide

The hide is grey and lichened like an old rock, and under the hand it is as cold as one.

Integument of near-mineral density; lichens colonize it.

The bones

The bones are as thick as a man's thigh at the wrist, and set close, like courses of masonry.

Massive skeleton adapted to load; dense trabecular structure.

The eyes

The eyes are small and deep, and they cannot bear even the grey before dawn.

Photosensitive; adapted to near-total darkness.

Its Habits

It does nothing at all that the reader can prompt. By day it hardens the moment it comes into the light of the page; by night it breathes, slowly, and its breath clouds in the cold.Entry into view is the only input. Day: mineralization sequence. Night: slow breathing with visible vapour.

While the sun is up in the reader's sky, the Troll is struck to stone the moment it is seen, and it stays stone until the sun goes down.While the sun's altitude is above -0.833 degrees at the reader's location, the specimen mineralizes on entering view and remains so until local sunset.

Nothing need be done; it keeps its own hours.No action required by keyboard or touch. With reduced motion two still plates show it: stone by day, and breathing by night.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Orc. The Orc also comes from the deep, but it is a host grown up out of the soil. The Troll is one body, old and slow, and the sun alone can hold it.Both are Second Tier flesh. The Orc is a swarm; the Troll is solitary and locked by sunlight.
