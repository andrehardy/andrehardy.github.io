The Account

The Lich is a sorcerer who has hidden his death outside his body, so that the body may be cut down and burned and still rise, until the hiding place is found.

A practitioner of the Second Tier who has transferred the life out of the body into a stone or vessel, leaving the body as an animated husk that cannot be killed while the vessel is whole.

I

In the Russian tales that Alexander Afanasyev gathered in the 1850s, there is a sorcerer called Koschei the Deathless. He is old and thin and terrible, and he steals brides, and he cannot be killed, because his death is not in him. In the tale of the Frog Princess, the witch Baba Yaga tells Prince Ivan where it is. Koschei’s death is at the end of a needle. The needle is in an egg, the egg in a duck, the duck in a hare, and the hare in a chest at the top of a tall oak. A bear pulls down the oak, a drake strikes the duck so that it drops the egg, and a pike brings the egg out of the sea. Ivan breaks off the point of the needle, and Koschei dies.

Of its kinds the tales and the books name several: the deathless sorcerer, the giant with no heart in his body, the lich of the modern tales of sword and spell, who keeps his life in a box. The Registry counts them as one. All of them hold the same secret, and all of them can be undone in the same way.

It is found where it has chosen to wait: in a crypt, a tower, a kingdom past all maps. The body sits on its throne or lies on its bier. The life is somewhere else, and that place is always hard to reach.

Of its body this much is certain: it is not where the life is. The body can be cut and burned and scattered, and it will be raised again. Only the hidden vessel matters, and whatever is in the vessel is still alive.

It is not to be fought, but searched for. A hero who strikes at the body is wasting his strength. The way to end a lich is to find where it has hidden its death.

Body: human, dead and dried, moved by the practitioner's will from a distance. It is the decoy, not the organ of life. Source: the Second Tier; the life is housed in dense mineral (a stone, a needle, an egg, a box) placed far from the body. Mechanism: damage to the body is repaired or replaced; destruction of the vessel ends the practitioner at once, wherever the body is. Behaviour: patient, solitary, and intent on hiding the vessel. Containment: find and break the vessel. Hazard to the observer: grave near the body; slight near the vessel, which cannot act on its own.

Under the Screen

The husk

The body is a skeleton in dried skin and old silk. Under the screen there is nothing in it: no heart, no light, no warmth.

The body shows no internal activity under the fluoroscope.

The vessel

The stone in the margin glows under the screen with a cold green light, and something in it beats.

The vessel is the only active structure; it pulses at a regular interval.

The script

On the walls of the reliquary is writing in a script that nobody now reads. It becomes plain only to those who have found the stone.

The case text is illegible until the vessel has been located.

Its Habits

The body is the decoy. While attention rests on it, the writing stays dead. Find the stone in the margin, and the writing becomes plain.The passage is rendered in an unreadable script until the reader's hand or focus reaches the vessel. The true text is always in the page beneath.

The longer the reader has stayed away, the thicker the dust on the glass of the reliquary. The stone's light never changes.Dust on the case increases with time since the room was last seen. The vessel's light is constant.

Come straight to the stone, and the words are given up at once.Keyboard focus on the vessel, or anywhere inside the passage, renders the text plainly; by touch, a press on the vessel. With reduced motion the text is shown plainly.

  • The body, lying still, with the writing dead around it.Attention on the husk; text unreadable.
  • The stone found, and the writing plain.Vessel located; text rendered plainly.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Vampire. The Vampire also cheats the grave, but it caught its death like a fever. The Lich chose its death, and put it where no one would look.Both are undead. The Vampire is an infection; the Lich is a practitioner's deliberate transfer of life into a vessel.
