The Minotaur

The Bull Walled in by ArtA failed graft of man and bull, held in a cooling house of stone

The Account

The Minotaur is a man with the head of a bull, born of a queen's unnatural passion and shut by her husband in a house of passages that the craftsman Daedalus built to hold it.

A Grafted body joining human and bovine tissue, made by human craft rather than drawn from either Source, which is unstable and must be held in a structure that takes up its heat.

M

Minos, king of Crete, asked Poseidon for a bull out of the sea to prove his right to the throne, and vowed to sacrifice it. The bull came, white and beautiful, and Minos kept it and sacrificed another. Poseidon’s revenge fell on the queen, Pasiphaë, who was made to desire the bull. The craftsman Daedalus built her a hollow cow of wood, and the child she bore was a man with a bull’s head. They called him Asterion, the starry one, but he is known by the name of the queen’s husband: the bull of Minos.

Of its kind there was only one, and the Registry counts it alone.

It was found at Knossos, in Crete, in the house of passages that Daedalus built to hold it. The house was so made that whoever entered it could never find the way out. Into it, every ninth year as Ovid tells it, Athens sent seven youths and seven maidens as tribute, and none of them came out until Theseus went in with Ariadne’s thread.

Of its body this much is certain: it was not meant. It was made by craft, from a passion that the gods sent, and it ran hot and wild, at war with itself. The walls were its keeping as much as its prison. Borges, who gave it its own voice in 1947, has it live alone in a house with no locks, and wait for the one who will come to free it.

It is not to be approached inside its walls, nor let out. The thread is the only safe road in. The Labyrinth is not cruel to it, for the walls are the only thing that keeps it calm.

Body: human trunk and limbs with a bull's head and neck; great strength. Source: neither Tier; the graft is the work of craft (Daedalus's device), and nothing sustains it but its own metabolism. Mechanism: the graft does not settle. The body runs hot and the joined tissues are at war; agitation shows as tremor that builds to a violent fit. Containment: a structure of many thick stone walls, which bleeds off its heat and confines its movement; the deeper the plan, the calmer the body. Diet: recorded as human, every nine years. Hazard to the observer: lethal inside the walls; nil outside them.

Its Habits

Reading is building. Each passage read lays down another course of the Labyrinth, and the more walls there are, the quieter the thing at the centre. A narrow page holds it best.Reading progress draws the walls of the plan. More walls, less tremor. A narrow view counts as tighter containment. The tremor stays inside the case.

While the sun is in the Bull, from about the twentieth of April to the twentieth of May, it is restless, and the trembling is quicker to start.While the sun's ecliptic longitude is between 30 and 60 degrees, tremor onset is faster. Its amplitude does not change.

Nothing need be done but read; the walls rise of themselves.Keyboard and touch readers build the walls by reading. With reduced motion the walls are drawn complete and there is no tremor.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Golem. The Golem is also the work of a maker's hands, but it obeys. The Minotaur was a graft that failed, and could only be walled in.Both are human work. The Golem is obedient and stoppable; the Minotaur is unstable and must be contained.
