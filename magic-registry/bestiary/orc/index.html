The Account

The Orc is not born but comes up out of broken ground, full-grown and armed, as mushrooms come up after rain, and there is never only one.

A Chimeric humanoid that is the fruiting body of an underground fungal web turned by the Second Tier, grown in soil to adult size and produced in great numbers.

T

The word is older than the creature as it is now known. The poet of Beowulf set the orcneas among the brood of Cain, with the giants and the elves, and the glossaries of the Old English schoolmasters gave orc for a monster of the underworld and for the god of the dead. It was J. R. R. Tolkien, a professor of that language, who took the old word and gave it a body. In his books orcs live in the mines and fortresses of the mountains, hate the sun, and serve whatever strong hand will drive them.

Of their kinds the tellers name many: orcs, goblins, the great Uruk-hai, and, in the tales of the far future, the orks that come up out of the soil from spores. The Registry counts them as one, for they share one origin, whatever size they are grown to.

They are found in broken ground: spoil heaps, mines, battlefields, the trodden mud of an old camp. Wherever the earth has been cut open and left wet, the host may come up.

Of their bodies this much is certain: they are grown, not born. Below the ground runs a web of fine white threads, as in any wood where mushrooms grow, and from it, when the season and the ground allow, pods swell in the soil. Each pod opens on a body already grown and already angry. When one dies it breaks into dust, and the dust settles into the ground to begin again.

They are not to be fought on wet ground, nor left unburned when killed. The old soldiers’ rule is the only rule: dry the field and burn the dead. Any ground they have bled on is their nursery.

Body: humanoid, heavily muscled, with tough grey-green hide over tissue that is part fungal. Source: the Second Tier, through a mycelial web in the soil. Mechanism: the web spreads through disturbed ground and, when conditions allow, fruits: pods form below the surface and each yields an adult in days. Individuals shed spores at death, so every battlefield seeds the next host. Trigger: broken and wetted ground, and autumn. Behaviour: aggressive, numerous, loosely led by the largest. Containment: dry the ground, burn the dead, and never leave a field of them unburned. Hazard to the observer: high in numbers; the spores are inert to humans.

Under the Screen

The hide

The hide is thick, grey-green and cool, with a bloom on it like the cap of a field mushroom.

Tough integument with a fungal surface layer.

The threads

From the soles and the spine fine white threads run down into the ground, so that while it stands still it is still, in some sense, rooted.

Hyphal strands connect the body to the web while it is at rest on soil.

The spore sacs

Under the arms and along the ribs are sacs of fine dust, which break when it dies and go up like smoke from a puffball.

Spore reservoirs rupture at death and disperse widely.

Its Habits

Whoever lingers over a passage finds it overgrown: the threads creep out into the margins and between the lines. Moving on quickly tears them, and the page is clean again.Dwell time grows threads into the margins and line spacing of the passage. Fast reading onward breaks them. The text itself is never covered.

In autumn, the fruiting season in the reader's half of the world, the threads run faster and more pods swell in the soil.During local autumn (by hemisphere) web growth is faster and more pods are present.

Read still, and the threads grow slowly; read on, and they part. The words are never hidden under them.Keyboard and touch readers see the same growth from stillness. With reduced motion two still plates show the soil: the pod developing, and the individual emerged.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Troll. The Troll also belongs to the deep, but it is one great body that the sun locks still. The Orc is many small bodies that the soil keeps sending up.Both are Second Tier flesh. The Troll is solitary and sun-sensitive; the Orc is a swarm grown from fungus.
