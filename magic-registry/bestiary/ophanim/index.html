The Account

The Ophanim are the wheels the prophet saw by the river, a wheel within a wheel, their rims full of eyes, which go wherever the spirit goes and never turn as they go.

A bodiless order of the Sealed Light, interlocked rings of radiance covered in organs of sight, that carry and observe and that no practitioner's art can reach.

I

In the fifth year of the exile of King Jehoiachin, the priest Ezekiel was among the captives by the river Chebar, in the land of the Chaldeans, when the heavens opened. He saw a storm out of the north, a great cloud and a fire folding in on itself, and in it four living creatures. Beside each creature was a wheel. The wheels were the colour of chrysolite, and each was made as a wheel within a wheel. Their rims were high and dreadful and full of eyes all round. They went in any of four directions without turning, and where the spirit went, they went.

Of their kind the later writers made an order. The Book of Enoch sets the Ophanim with the Cherubim and the Seraphim, who never sleep and guard the throne. The Christian writers called them Thrones, and Dante places them in the third circle about the point of light, after the Seraphim and the Cherubim. The Registry counts them as one.

They are found where the Sealed Light is, and nowhere else. They are not summoned, and the accounts do not record that anyone has ever gone looking for them. They come to the prophet; the prophet does not go to them.

Of their bodies this much is certain: they have none that can be dissected. There are rims and fire and eyes, and nothing holds them together but the spirit that is in them. The prophet uses the word “likeness” again and again, as though what he saw had no proper name.

They are not to be looked at long. They do no harm, but the eye fails before them. The prophet fell on his face.

Body: none of flesh. Two or more rings set at right angles to one another, of burning metal or light, their rims lined with eyes. Source: the Sealed Light, which no Tier draws on. Mechanism: the rings move in any direction without turning and keep station with the presence they carry. The eyes observe without pause. Behaviour: they do not act on the observer; they attend. Containment: none known and none attempted. Hazard to the observer: the brightness only; no account records harm from the wheels themselves.

Under the Screen

The rims

The rims are high and dreadful, as the prophet said, and so bright that the reticle's hairs vanish across them.

The rings are of great size and brightness; the instrument's own markings are lost against them.

The eyes

The eyes are set close all round the rims, without lids, and every one of them is looking at the observer.

Lidless organs of sight cover the rings; all orient toward the viewer.

The axle

There is no axle. The wheels hold together by something that the glass cannot show, and the screen shows nothing at all.

No structure joins the rings; the fluoroscope shows no interior.

Its Habits

The specimen answers nearness: wherever the reader's hand is, every eye on every rim turns to it. The wheels themselves stay in the middle of the glass while the words pass through.All eyes track the reader's hand. The rings stay centred in the view as the passage moves past them.

Within an hour of noon, when the sun stands highest in the reader's sky, the rims blaze, but with a steady fire and never a flicker. At night only the eyes burn.Within one hour of local solar noon the rings glow at full intensity, held steady. At night only the eyes are lit.

Whatever the reader attends to, they attend to as well.Keyboard focus is tracked as the hand is; by touch, the point of contact. With reduced motion the wheels are still and the eyes look forward.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Djinn. The Djinn is also fire without flesh, but it is bound by a seal and plays with chance. The Ophanim are bound by nothing and play with nothing; they carry the Sealed Light.Both are bodiless fire. The Djinn is bound and threshold-born; the Ophanim are of the Sealed Light and unbound.
