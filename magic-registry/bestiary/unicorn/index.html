The Unicorn

The Warden of the SpringA pristine beast whose horn clears poison from water

The Account

The Unicorn is a white beast of the deep forest with one horn, which it dips in springs to make them clean, and which no hunter can take while it is wary.

A Pristine animal bound to clean water through the Grid, whose horn neutralizes poison, and which shows itself only to a calm and unthreatening presence.

T

The Greek physician Ctesias, who served at the Persian court, wrote around 400 BC of wild asses in India as large as horses, white in body, with a horn on the forehead a cubit and a half long. Those who drink from cups made of the horn, he said, are safe from poison and from the falling sickness. Every later writer on the unicorn begins with him.

Of its kinds the books give several. There is the swift wild ass of India and the monoceros of Pliny, with a stag’s head, an elephant’s feet and a boar’s tail. The bestiaries give a small white beast like a kid. The Registry counts one, for they agree in the horn.

It is found in deep forest near clean water. The Physiologus, the book of beasts that the Middle Ages copied without end, says that no hunter can catch it by force, but that it will come of its own accord and lay its head in the lap of a maiden who sits alone in the wood. In the great tapestries of the Hunt, woven at the end of the fifteenth century, the hunters find it kneeling at a fountain, dipping its horn in the water to make it clean, while the other beasts of the wood wait to drink.

Of its body this much is certain: the horn is the whole of its virtue. The courts of Europe paid more than its weight in gold for horns that were said to be its own, and tested their wine with them. Most of these horns came from the northern seas, from the tusk of the narwhal. Its gift is that poison cannot stand near it, and it gives that gift freely to any spring it drinks from.

It is not to be hunted, nor startled, nor approached in a hurry. Those who would see it must sit still at the edge of the wood at first light.

Body: equine or caprine in build, white, with a single spiralled horn from the brow and cloven hooves. Source: the First Tier, through the Grid, by way of running water. Mechanism: the horn detects and neutralizes poison in water and in any vessel it touches; where it drinks, the spring runs clean downstream. It does not age. Behaviour: shy; it flees or attacks at any sudden or erratic movement, and approaches only stillness. Containment: impossible by force; the old accounts take it only by stillness. Hazard to the observer: slight while still; grave if it is startled at close range.

Its Habits

It does not show itself to a restless eye. Read slowly and stay still, and it steps out of the trees. Move about, and it lowers its horn, and its glare fills the case.Low movement and slow reading raise a reveal measure. Erratic movement brings a slow glare, held inside the case and never flashing.

At first light and for an hour after sunrise it comes down to drink, and shows itself sooner to the patient.From civil dawn to one hour after local sunrise the reveal threshold is lower.

Read still, and it will come.Keyboard and touch readers reveal it by reading at their own pace. With reduced motion a still plate shows it revealed.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Sidhe. The Sidhe also never age, and also wither where the land is spoiled. But the Sidhe keep the land, and the Unicorn keeps the water.Both are the Pristine. The Sidhe are bound to the land; the Unicorn to the water.
