The Account

The Djinn is a spirit made of smokeless fire, older than mankind, which can grant what is wished and take a terrible price, and which a seal of Solomon can shut in a bottle.

A bodiless force between the First and Second Tiers, swinging between chance and heat, that can bend outcomes when bound and is held only by a seal.

T

The Qur’an says that man was made from clay like the potter’s, and the jinn from smokeless fire. They are older than mankind, and they are many: some faithful and some not, some who listen at the gates of heaven and are driven off by shooting stars. They have their own nations and their own kings. Solomon, son of David, was given power over them, and set them to build his temple, and he sealed the disobedient in vessels of brass and threw them into the sea.

Of their kinds the tellers name many: the ifrit, the marid, the ghul of the desert, the jinni who serves the lamp or the ring. The Registry counts them as one, for they are all fire.

They are found in the empty places: deserts, ruins, old wells, the borders of cultivated land. Some live in the sea, sealed in brass where Solomon left them.

Of their bodies this much is certain: they have none that will stay. In the tale of the fisherman in the Thousand and One Nights, a poor fisherman draws up a brass bottle stopped with lead and stamped with the seal of Solomon. When he opens it, smoke pours out and becomes a jinni as tall as a tower. The jinni has sworn to kill whoever frees him, because he waited so long. The fisherman says that he cannot believe so great a jinni ever fitted in so small a bottle. The jinni turns back into smoke and goes in to show him, and the fisherman stops the bottle again.

It is not to be released, nor bargained with, nor asked for a wish without thought for the wording. A seal of Solomon should never be broken by a curious hand.

Body: none fixed. It appears as smoke, flame or any shape it chooses, at any size. Source: the threshold between the Tiers: from the First, the power to bend chance; from the Second, heat. Mechanism: bound by a seal and under command, it brings about what it is told to, read to the letter. Unbound, it acts on its own will, which is often hostile to whoever bound it. Signs: heat without fuel; smoke that will not disperse. Containment: a sealed vessel of metal stamped with the seal of Solomon. Hazard to the observer: grave when it is released; a wish is a hazard in itself.

Under the Screen

The fire

The fire burns with no smoke and no fuel, the clear blue of the hottest part of a flame.

Combustion without fuel or smoke; blue-white, very high temperature.

The smoke

What smoke there is comes only when it changes shape. It does not drift, and it does not thin.

Visible vapour occurs during transformation and does not disperse.

The seal

The seal is a star of two triangles, cut in the lead. Under the screen it is the only thing that holds still while everything inside moves.

The seal is the binding structure; the fluoroscope shows it fixed against the motion within.

Its Habits

Hold the vessel and it cracks. The longer it is held, the further the crack runs, and the print about the case bends in the heat. Let go, and the seal closes.A sustained press on the vessel opens a crack. Duration increases the warping of the room's type. Release reseals.

In the hour after sunset, at the prayer of the evening, the seal is weakest, and the crack in the lead runs faster.For one hour after local sunset the seal's resistance is lower and the crack propagates faster.

Hold the seal to break it, and let go to close it.Holding the space bar or the enter key on the vessel does the same; by touch, a long press. With reduced motion the sealed vessel is shown with its crack drawn.

  • Sealed, the brass warm and the lead whole.Contained.
  • Cracked, with smoke at the seal and fire behind it.Seal breached; partial release.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Ophanim. The Ophanim are also fire without flesh, but they are bound by nothing. The Djinn is bound, and every wish it grants is the price of its binding.Both are bodiless fire. The Ophanim are of the Sealed Light and unbound; the Djinn is of the threshold and held by a seal.
