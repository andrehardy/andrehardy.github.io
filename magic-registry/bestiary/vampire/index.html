The Vampire

The Cold Guest of the Grave A contagion that mends the body and steals its heat

The Account

The Vampire is a dead man who will not stay dead, kept from rotting by a sickness out of the deep and kept from freezing only by the warm blood of the living.

A human body taken over by a contagion from the Second Tier that repairs any wound but destroys the body's own heat, so that warmth must be taken from living blood.

T

The Serbs and the Vlachs had the word first, and the Austrian army’s doctors carried it west. Some years before, in the village of Medvegja on the Morava, a soldier named Arnold Paole had broken his neck in a fall from a hay wagon, and afterwards four of his neighbours said he had come back to plague them. In the winter of 1731 the dead began to walk there again, and in January 1732 the regimental surgeon Johann Flückinger was sent with a commission to look. They opened the graves. They found bodies that had not rotted, with fresh blood in them and new skin under the old, and they cut off the heads and burned the bodies, and wrote it all down and signed it. His report was read in Vienna, and then in Paris, and the vampire entered the books of learned men.

Of its kinds there are as many as there are valleys in the Carpathians, but the Registry counts one. It is a man or woman who has died and been taken over by something from below. Whatever it is keeps the body whole and mends it of any hurt, but it cannot keep the body warm.

It is found in vaults, in barrows, in the cold cellars of old houses, wherever earth and stone keep it cool and hidden through the day. It is said to need the earth of its own country, and the accounts agree that it keeps to the dark.

Of its body this much is certain: it is cold. The heart barely moves, the blood is thick and slow, and the nerves, starved of warmth, fail by degrees. Warm blood is the only thing that starts the body again, and for a few hours after it has fed the vampire is flushed and strong and nearly alive. Then the cold comes back.

It is not to be met at night, and not to be met at all without the old remedies. Those remedies are the end of any body. The stake pins it, fire consumes it, and the severed head stops it. The thing that lived in it has nowhere else to go.

Body: human, after death or near it, colonized by a contagion from below. Source: the Second Tier. Mechanism: the contagion halts decay and closes wounds almost at once, but the body can no longer generate heat. Its temperature falls toward that of its surroundings, and its nerves fail in the cold. Fresh blood restores heat for a few hours, and the body seeks it. Signs: no warmth at rest; a flush after feeding; aversion to direct sun, which overheats the colonized skin. Containment: earth, cold and confinement by day. The old remedies (stake, fire, the severed head) all end the body the contagion lives in. Hazard to the observer: high at close range, and highest between midnight and first light.

Under the Screen

The heart

The heart is still more often than it beats. When it beats, it beats hard, and the whole body flushes from it like an ember blown upon.

Cardiac activity is intermittent at rest and forceful after feeding.

The veins

The veins stand out dark against the pale skin, like roots in snow, and run hot for a moment when there is blood in them.

Vessels are prominent and visible; they show as the hottest part of the body after feeding.

The skin

The skin is unspoiled long after it should have spoiled, and burns in sunlight as damp paper burns, slowly and then all at once.

Tissue does not decay. Direct sunlight overheats the colonized skin and causes progressive burns.

Its Habits

It answers nearness. Whatever warmth comes close, it turns its head toward; a warmth that lingers it drinks, and the crypt grows darker by what it has taken.The reader's hand is read as a heat source. The head tracks it; within the body's outline the veins flare; a long dwell drains the room's light until the source withdraws.

Between midnight and first light the thing in the crypt is awake, and the warmth it takes is taken faster and from further off.From local midnight to civil dawn the room is in its active state: heat is drawn faster and from a wider radius.

Keep at a distance and it lies still. To bring it near without a hand, attend to it directly; to let it rest, look away.Keyboard focus on the specimen counts as nearness. A long press does the same by touch. With reduced motion a single plate shows the body mid-flare.

  • Cold, and nearly not there at all: a dim outline in the black.At rest, near ambient temperature. Barely visible to the instrument.
  • Flushed, with the veins burning orange and the heart going.After taking heat. Veins at peak temperature, heart active.
  • Awake in the small hours, when it takes more and takes it faster.Active state, from midnight to first light. Faster and wider heat draw.

Its Nearest Neighbour

The Lich. The Lich also cheats the grave, but by its own art and its own choosing. The Vampire did not choose; it caught its death and keeps it.The Lich is a practitioner's deliberate act with its life held in an object. The Vampire is an infection that keeps a body going.
