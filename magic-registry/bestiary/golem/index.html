The Account

The Golem is a man of river clay, shaped by a rabbi and woken by the word for truth, and one letter rubbed out of that word lays it down dead.

T

The Talmud tells, in a few lines, that the sage Rava made a man and sent him to Rabbi Zeira. The rabbi spoke to him, and he did not answer, and Rabbi Zeira knew him then for the work of the magicians and told him to return to his dust. The book says no more, but the later scholars of the Sefer Yetzirah, the Book of Creation, found in it the means: the letters of the holy names, combined in the right order over clay.

Of its kinds there are few, for it is rarely made, and the Registry counts one. In 1808 Jacob Grimm set down what the Jews of Poland told: that a master would shape a man from clay and speak the name of God over it, and write emet, truth, on its brow. The man would work, but grow every day, until his maker could no longer reach his forehead. One master, to stop his, asked the golem to stoop and pull off his boots, and rubbed out the first letter as he bent. Emet became met, dead, and the clay fell on its maker and crushed him.

It is found in Prague above all, where the tale has come to rest on Rabbi Judah Loew, and his golem is said to lie still in the attic of the Old-New Synagogue. The rabbi stilled it each Friday before the Sabbath. Once he forgot, and it ran wild in the Jewish Town until he caught it at the door of the synagogue and drew the name out of its mouth.

Of its body this much is certain: it is clay, and only clay. It does not eat or sleep or speak. It works, it grows, and it obeys, and it is moved by nothing but the word.

It is not to be left running, nor grown too tall, nor trusted to stop by itself. The word that wakes it must always be within reach of the hand that wrote it.

Body: human-shaped, modelled in clay, larger and heavier than a man; no organs. Source: the Second Tier, carried by a word: the word emet (truth) inscribed on the brow, or a written name placed in the mouth, sets the clay resonating with the deep. Mechanism: while the word stands, the clay moves and obeys its maker. It cannot speak and does not think. It grows with time, and grows harder to control. Stopping: remove the first letter, leaving met (dead), or take the name from its mouth; the clay goes still. Containment: an iron cage and a rest kept every seventh day. Hazard to the observer: low while tended; grave if left running.

Under the Screen

The brow

On the brow are three letters cut into the clay: aleph, mem, tav, which spell the word for truth. Under the screen they show as three bright lines, and nothing else in the body is lit.

Its Habits

It wakes to friction. Rubbed back and forth, the clay warms and sparks; when the charge is enough, the word lights and it opens its eyes.Rapid alternating movement over the specimen builds charge. At threshold the inscription lights and the figure activates.

From sunset on Friday to nightfall on Saturday, by the reader's own sky, it is at rest, as the rabbi of Prague kept it, and nothing will wake it.From local sunset on Friday until three stars on Saturday the specimen is held dormant and no input will activate it.

To wake it without the hand, rock the arrow keys from side to side over it; by touch, rub it.With focus on the vessel, alternating the left and right arrow keys builds the same charge. With reduced motion two still plates show it asleep and awake.
