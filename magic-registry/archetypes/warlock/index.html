  1. I.Of the PartiesSummary

    The Warlock was born with nothing, learned nothing and brews nothing, and holds great power all the same, because a power under the earth has lent it in return for service and a debt that will one day be called.

  2. II.Of the TermsHow the power works

    The Warlock is the one practitioner who began with nothing. No blood of the deep runs in the Warlock’s veins; no book was mastered and no pot was ever tended. A Warlock is an ordinary person who went at night to the place where roads cross, called up something that was waiting beneath it, and struck a bargain.

    The bargain is the whole of the art. What lives under the Mantle is old and patient, and it wants hands on the surface. In return it lends what it has: a skin that blades turn from, a word that makes the floor fall away, fortune, rank, a lifespan longer than the others in the churchyard. The Warlock speaks, and something far below answers. Every power is a clause, and every clause was written by the other side.

    The service is not light. A Warlock owes errands: wild ground smothered under stone, iron and blood gathered, the works of the sky’s servants troubled. A Warlock who forgets is reminded. And beneath the service runs the term, ten years or twenty or a long life, at the end of which the debt is called and the hounds come down the road.

    Some Warlocks are clever. They read every line, find the word that can be bent, and set one creditor against another until both are ruined and the Warlock walks away. The Registry records such escapes, and records how rare they are.

    What a Warlock cannot do is strike against the sky’s own favour. A blow from the deep is still a blow, and one aimed at a Paladin in the midst of the vow goes astray like all the rest.

    Being: a baseline human. Source: the Second Tier, by agreement. Access: Contract.

    A Warlock has no inherited capacity, no learned technique and no prepared substances. The power is borrowed. Through a prescribed ritual at a prescribed kind of place, the Warlock makes contact with an agent of the Second Tier and agrees terms: a Covenant. The agent then supplies what was agreed, which may be physical protection, force applied on request, wealth and standing, or a prolonged life.

    The Warlock pays in two ways. During the term the Warlock serves the Second Tier's interests on the surface: smothering living ground that carries the Grid, gathering iron and blood, and disrupting the First Tier's agents. At the end of the term a final payment falls due, usually the Warlock's life.

    The Warlock does not perform the effects. When the Warlock invokes a clause, the agent acts, often from deep underground: the ground shakes, the floor gives way, a blow is turned. This is why a Warlock can achieve great force from an ordinary body.

    Limits: the power is only what the terms specify, read literally. It can be withdrawn if the service lapses. The final payment cannot be avoided except through flaws in the text. Force from below is still harm, and probability shielding turns it aside.

  5. V.Of Other PartiesCompared with

    The Paladin

    Both may walk out of a slaughter without a scratch, and the crowd cannot tell them apart. But the Paladin was spared because the sky would have the vow kept, and the Warlock because the deep was paid. The Paladin can lose the favour only by betraying it; the Warlock loses the protection on a date already written down.

    Both can be protected from harm, on opposite terms. The Paladin's field comes from the First Tier and lasts while the Paladin serves its purpose; the Warlock's protection comes from the Second Tier under a Covenant and lasts until the term ends or the service lapses.
