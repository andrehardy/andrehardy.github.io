The Root of ItSummary

The Druid is the keeper of a grove who lays a hand on the living web beneath it and gives that web work to do, and for all the bark and moss, the Druid's power comes down from the sky.

A Druid directs the Grid, the First Tier's fungal web through living things, to make organisms grow, spread or act, and can do nothing where nothing lives.

The Account of the WebHow the power works

Under every field and forest runs a web finer than hair and older than the oldest oak, joining root to root and creature to creature. The Druid is its keeper.

That is the first thing to know about the Druid, and the thing most often missed. For the Druid wears bark and moss, keeps to the woods, and is reckoned the very spirit of the soil. Yet the web the Druid tends answers to the sky. The Vault runs through it as sap runs through a tree, and when the Druid lays a hand upon the web, it is the sky that hears.

The work is slow beyond any patience but a tree’s. A keeper plants a grove and listens to it for a lifetime; the web grows thick beneath, learns the keeper’s step, and in the end does the keeper’s bidding: the bramble closing a road, the ivy pulling down a wall, the wolves leaving one valley for the next. Nothing is made from nothing. Whatever the Druid commands must already be there, and alive, and it does the work with its own strength.

The old tales give the Druid the bear’s shape and the eagle’s, but the Druid’s art goes outward from the Druid and never inward. Flesh that changes its own form belongs to the Chimeric, and the Registry files it there.

What lives is the Druid’s to ask, and what does not, is not. On bare stone, at sea or in a hall of dressed granite, the keeper is an old walker leaning on a stick; and the dead, who have left the web, are past any keeping at all.

Being: a baseline human or Tier III Gamma. Source: the First Tier, through the Grid. Access: Tending the Grid.

The Druid looks like the most earthly of practitioners and is not. The Grid, the planet-wide fungal web that links roots, soil and living things, carries the First Tier, and a Druid who has tended a place long enough can direct that web. Directed, it acts through the organisms it reaches: roots grow quickly where they are told, vines climb, spores bloom, and animals come or go.

Everything a Druid does is done by something alive. A Druid does not grow a vine out of nothing; the vine must already be there, or its seed must be in the soil. The power scales with how much life is present and how long it has been cultivated, which is why the grove matters.

Fiction often lets Druids take animal shapes. A body that changes its own form belongs to Tier III Alpha, and the Registry files that ability there; the Druid's art acts on other organisms, never on the Druid's own body.

Limits: no living organisms means no power. Forced growth exhausts the soil and the creatures around it. Dead tissue lies outside the Grid, so the reanimated dead cannot be touched.

What It Takes, and What It Cannot ReachCosts and blind spots

What it takesCosts

  1. Patience past a lifetime. Any living thing within reach will answer the Druid at once, but only in its own small measure; to close a road with bramble or turn the wolves from a valley, the web must be tended for decades, and a keeper's work is often finished by the next.

The Neighbouring GroundCompared with

The Shaman

The Shaman and the Druid sit at either end of the same web. The Shaman eats the key and listens to it; the Druid tends it and is listened to. Both look like children of the soil and both answer to the sky, and between them they are why the Registry warns its readers never to file a practitioner by the mud on the boots.

Both draw on the First Tier through the Grid. The Shaman's access is perception and changes nothing; the Druid's is direction, acting through living organisms. Both are filed under the First Tier despite their earthly appearance.

The Witch

Neighbours in the same wood, and strangers under it. The Witch digs into the soil for what the deep has put there, the poison in the root and the metal in the stone; the Druid tends the living web that runs above all that and answers to the sky. Both gather and brew, and both are called wise by villages that cannot tell a cure from a curse. The Registry can: one art reaches down, and the other is reached down to.

Both work with plants, soil and animals, from opposite sources. The Witch extracts chemistry belonging to the Second Tier; the Druid directs the Grid, which belongs to the First. A Witch's poison acts on a body directly; a Druid acts only through living organisms that the Grid reaches.

In the TrialsTrial results

The Glass upon the TableTrial: bring down a glass

Where there is living green in the room a tendril sweeps the glass away; where there is none, nothing happens.

Everything depends on the room. If ivy has crept in at the window, or the old boards still hold a thread of living mould, the Druid lays a palm on the floor and asks; a tendril lengthens across the table in the space of a breath and lifts the glass over the edge. In a room of dressed stone and clean plaster the Druid asks, and nothing answers.

The Druid can act only through living organisms. If plants or fungus are present, the Druid directs the Grid to speed their growth across the table until they push the glass off. In a sterile room there is nothing to direct, and the attempt fails.

The Stranger in the ChairTrial: empty an occupied chair

The stranger's own breath turns against them, and the chair is emptied.

The Druid needs no ivy here, for a living body is itself a meadow. Every breath carries spores, and they lie in the stranger's chest as seed lies in a furrow. The Druid asks them to wake. They wake all at once, and the stranger, fighting for air, falls from the chair and does not rise again.

A human body holds microorganisms, including fungal spores in the airways. The Druid directs the Grid to make those spores grow rapidly in the person's lungs, and breathing fails within moments. The chair is emptied, fatally, and the Druid acted only through organisms that were already there.

The Na'vi

Avatar, 2009

Called “Na'vi” in the work; filed here as the Druid.

The Na'vi are the Registry's model Druids, because they bind themselves to a living web that joins every root and creature of their world, and that web answers when they call.

The Na'vi are filed as Druids because they act through a planet-wide biological web that links living things, bonding with creatures and finally rousing the whole web to act.

The Na'vi are filed as Druids because they act through a planet-wide biological web that links living things, bonding with creatures and finally rousing the whole web to act.

The Na'vi are the Registry's model Druids, because they bind themselves to a living web that joins every root and creature of their world, and that web answers when they call.

Pandora is one great grove. Beneath its forests the roots of every tree are joined to one another, a web finer and wider than any keeper on this world could tend, and the Na'vi are its keepers. Each of them can join to it in the flesh, to a mount, to a great bird, to the sacred trees where the voices of the dead are kept. They do not cast and they do not command by force; they ask, through the web, and living things do the work. At the last, when the web itself is threatened, it rises, and the beasts of the whole forest come to fight. That is the Druid's art, writ as large as a world.

In the film, the scientist Grace Augustine discovers that the trees of Pandora are linked through their roots in a network of signals, a planet-wide biological web the Na'vi call Eywa. The Na'vi physically bond with animals and trees through a neural link at the end of their braids, which lets them ride and direct creatures and hear the memories held in the sacred trees. In the climax, Eywa sends the planet's animals to fight the invaders. Acting on the world through living organisms joined in a web is the Druid's access, Tending the Grid.

The runner-up · Comic

Swamp Thing

Saga of the Swamp Thing, 1984

Called “a plant elemental” in the work; filed here as the Druid.

The Swamp Thing is the Registry's runner-up Druid, because every root and leaf on the earth answers him, though he is less the keeper of the living web than a thread of it that learned to walk.

The Swamp Thing is filed as a Druid because he acts through a world-wide web of plant life, directing growth at will, although he is himself made of that web rather than tending it.

The Swamp Thing is the Registry's runner-up Druid, because every root and leaf on the earth answers him, though he is less the keeper of the living web than a thread of it that learned to walk.

He believed himself a drowned scientist, until it was shown that he was a mass of swamp plants that had taken in the scientist's memories and made a man-shape out of them. What he truly is, is the green itself, standing up. He can bid a forest to grow over a road in a night, call the plants of a whole city to his aid, and leave one body of moss to rise in another half a world away, because every growing thing is joined to every other. The Registry ranks him second because the Druid keeps the web and stands apart from it, and the Swamp Thing is the web.

Alan Moore's run reveals that the Swamp Thing is not Alec Holland transformed but plant matter that absorbed Holland's consciousness. He is connected to the Green, the shared force of all plant life, and through it he can direct plant growth anywhere and grow a new body wherever vegetation exists. Acting on the world through a connected web of living organisms matches the Druid's access. The Registry ranks him below the exemplar because the true Druid is a person who tends the web, while the Swamp Thing is a part of it.

Nausicaä

Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind, 1984

Called “princess of the Valley of the Wind” in the work; filed here as the Druid.

Nausicaä is filed among the Druids because she tends the poisoned forest in secret until it shows her what it is, and the great beasts of that forest answer her.

Nausicaä is filed as a Druid because her influence works through living things, the plants she cultivates and the creatures she calms, rather than through any force of her own.

A thousand years after fire ended the old world, a forest of poison spreads over the earth, and its spores kill whoever breathes them. The princess of a small valley gathers those spores and grows them in a hidden room beneath the castle, watered from a deep well, where they grow green and harmless. Fallen into the depths under the forest, she finds the air sweet and the sand clean: the forest is not the poison but the cure, drawing it out of the ruined soil. She calms the vast armoured insects with a whirling charm and her own hand, and in the end the herd she stood against lifts her up on golden threads. The Registry files her with the Druids, the keepers who wait for the living web to answer.

Nausicaä grows its plants in a secret garden beneath the castle, using clean water and soil from deep below, and they release no poison, which shows her that the poison lies in the soil. Beneath the jungle she finds clean air and sand: the jungle is purifying the earth. She calms the Ohmu and other giant insects with an insect charm, and at the end the Ohmu herd she stopped heals her with their golden tendrils.
