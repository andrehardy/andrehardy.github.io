No. iWhat She IsSummary

The Witch digs for what the deep earth has hidden in root, metal and blood, and boils it down until it will do harm or good on command; nothing she does comes from the sky, and nothing comes without the pot.

A Witch is an ordinary person who extracts and concentrates the chemistry of the Second Tier, toxins, minerals and blood, and acts on bodies only through what has been prepared in advance.

No. iiThe Account of the PotHow the power works

The Witch keeps her power on shelves. Root and bark hang drying from the rafters; jars of seed, of shaved metal and of things better left unnamed line the walls; the pot is never quite cold. Everything she can do, she made, and she made it from the earth.

This is what separates her from every practitioner of the sky. The Wizard reckons and the Shaman listens, but the Witch digs. The Mantle has put its strength into the heavy things, the lead in the stone, the poison in the nightshade root, the iron in the blood, and the Witch’s whole art is the drawing of it out: gathering, drying, steeping and boiling down, until one black drop holds what a whole hedgerow held.

What comes out of the pot works on the body, and works without regard for anyone’s will. It stills a limb, slows a heart, opens the eye to visions or closes it in sleep. It is carried on a breath, in a cup, on a needle, or by the cat that slips through a window the Witch could never pass. It never flies of its own accord, and it is never made in the moment. A Witch with empty shelves is only a woman in the woods.

The craft is paid for in the body that practises it. Years over the fumes leave their mark, and the line between the cure and the killing dose is drawn so fine that a village which sends for the wise woman at a birth will burn her after a death. She knows the measure better than anyone alive, and it is still not always enough.

Legend calls many women witches who were something else, and Morgan le Fay most of all. The Registry is stricter. A Witch is one who learned what the deep earth holds and labours to draw it out. One who carries that power already in her flesh is an Enchantress, and is filed there.

Being: a baseline human, or Tier III Gamma. Source: the Second Tier, through physical material. Access: Chemistry.

The Witch is the Registry's chemist. The Second Tier is the source of mass, minerals and the chemistry of living bodies, and a Witch draws on it the slow way: by gathering plants, fungi, metals and blood, and boiling, drying and reducing them until their active substances are concentrated. Belladonna, henbane, foxglove and certain fungi are the core of the craft, alongside lead, mercury and other heavy minerals.

The resulting compounds act directly on the body: they paralyse, sedate, stop the heart, cause visions or ease pain. A familiar is an ordinary animal kept under control by a compound in its food and used as a scout. Nothing a Witch does is projected at a distance by force of will; every effect needs a substance, and the substance must reach the target, by breath, by mouth, through the skin or by an animal carrier.

Fiction often merges the Witch with the Enchantress. The difference is the Being. A Witch is usually an ordinary person and must prepare everything outside the body. An Enchantress is Tier III and produces comparable effects from her own physiology, without preparation.

Limits: nothing works without preparation and stock. The compounds harm the maker too. Doses are narrow. A target who is never within reach, which is what a Wizard arranges, cannot be affected at all.

The oldest quarrel in the Registry, and the one legend made into Merlin and Morgan le Fay. The Witch works what is: flesh, root, metal and blood, the heavy things of the Mantle. The Wizard works what will happen. Her poison would kill him in a breath if she could ever find him in the room, and he makes sure that she never does. The Registry files Morgan herself as an Enchantress, but the quarrel is the Witch's own.

The Glass upon the TableTrial: bring down a glass

The glass falls, but only because the Witch made ready for it long before.

The Witch cannot shift a glass by looking at it, and would not waste the effort. She clicks her tongue once. From the rafters comes the rat she has fed from her own hand for a month, and it runs the length of the table and shoulders the glass over the edge. Had there been no rat, there would have been the table leg, painted a week ago with something that eats wood, and waiting only for weight.

In Shakespeare's Macbeth, the three witches, called the Weird Sisters, prophesy that Macbeth will become Thane of Cawdor and then king. In the fourth act they prepare a brew in a boiling cauldron from a long list of animal and plant ingredients, including eye of newt, toe of frog and root of hemlock, and from it raise apparitions that give Macbeth further prophecies. Their effects come from preparing and combining ingredients over time, which is the Witch's access, Chemistry. Their sight resembles a Shaman's, but they do not consume the brew; they work it, and the visions rise from the pot.

They meet on a blasted heath in thunder, and greet a victorious soldier with a title he does not yet hold, and then with the crown. When he comes back to them for more, they are at their cauldron in a dark cave, throwing in the ingredients one by one: poisoned entrails, fillet of a fenny snake, eye of newt and toe of frog, wool of bat and tongue of dog, root of hemlock dug in the dark, cooled at last with a baboon's blood. Out of that brew rise the apparitions that tell him what he longs to hear: an armed head, a bloody child, a child crowned with a tree in his hand. The Registry files them with the Witches: the art of the pot.

  • The three witches prophesy that Macbeth will be Thane of Cawdor and king hereafter.
  • They boil a brew of eye of newt, toe of frog, root of hemlock and many other ingredients in a cauldron.
  • From the cauldron they raise apparitions: an armed head, a bloody child, and a crowned child with a tree in his hand.
