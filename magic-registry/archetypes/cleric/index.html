The CreedSummary

The Cleric is an ordinary mortal entrusted with a light that no one may study, brew or buy, and through whose hands the flesh is returned to the shape it was first made in.

A Cleric is a baseline human permitted to relay the Sealed Broadcast, which restores living tissue to its original pattern and undoes what the other Tiers have done to it.

The Account of the LightHow the power works

No one reaches the Sealed Light. It reaches down, and it chooses. The Cleric is one whom it has found: an ordinary mortal, no older in blood than the miller or the ploughman, who has been weighed and judged fit to carry what cannot be made.

For the light above the Vault keeps its own counsel. The Wizard may reckon the sky and the Witch may brew the soil, but no sum unlocks this light, no cup holds it, and no bargain buys an hour of it. It is a hymn sung closed, whose words are known only to the choir, and it is lent to those who keep faith with it, from hand to hand down the long line of the entrusted.

What it does, it does to the flesh, and it does one thing only: it remembers. Laid on a wound, it recalls the skin as it was before the blade; laid on a fever, it recalls the blood before the sickness came. Nothing is added and nothing is forced. The body is shown its first pattern and returns to it, as a bent bough springs upright when the snow slides off.

So the light is gentle with the living and terrible to what the deep has made. A body worked from below, whether a corpse walked on strings or a face remade by the Mantle, has no first pattern that includes such workings. Shown the light, it unmakes them, and that unmaking is the fire the old painters set about the priest’s raised hand.

The office has plain rules. The light will not lift a cup or open a door, and against a healthy foe with a knife it is no use at all. It is lent and not given, and those who turn it to their own ends wake one morning with empty hands. The Cleric carries it as a candle is carried down a draughty nave: with care, for others, and never long enough to forget whose flame it is.

Being: a baseline human, vetted. Source: the Sealed Tier. Access: the Sealed Broadcast.

A Cleric generates nothing and calculates nothing. The Sealed Tier is a closed source above the First Tier; it cannot be reached by study, chemistry, inheritance or contract, and it answers only to carriers it has accepted. Those carriers relay it. The Cleric's part is to remain acceptable and to place the relay where it is needed: a hand on a wound, a symbol raised over a body, water carried into a sickroom.

What the Sealed Broadcast does to living tissue is restore it to its original, uncorrupted pattern. An infection clears, a wound closes and a growth recedes, because none of them belonged to the body as it was first made. Tissue that the Second Tier has altered, such as the flesh of a reanimated corpse or a body changed by the deep, is returned to its pattern too, and for those bodies the return is destructive. That is the whole of what fiction calls holy light: not heat and not force, but correction.

Limits: the relay has a single function and cannot be redirected to move objects, compel minds or predict events. It acts only on injury and corruption, so a healthy attacker is unaffected. Access can be withdrawn without warning from a carrier who misuses it, and relaying tires the body.

The holy symbol

Not a key but a seal of office: a sign worn at the breast to show what light its bearer answers to, as a herald's tabard shows a house. It opens nothing of itself, and in a thief's hand it is only silver.

An emblem of permission. It marks the carrier to the Sealed Tier and gives the relay a point to issue from, but it holds no power of its own; a stolen symbol does nothing.

The Paladin

They stand side by side in every chapel window and are mistaken for one another in every song, but they keep different gifts. The Cleric carries a light that heals what it touches; the Paladin wears a favour that turns aside what would touch him. One walks into the plague-house, and the other walks into the battle a step ahead of the first.

Both act for the upper sources, by different means. The Cleric relays the Sealed Broadcast, which restores tissue; the Paladin stands inside a First Tier field that makes harm improbable. The Cleric heals and cannot defend; the Paladin defends and cannot heal.

Leeloo

The Fifth Element, 1997

Called “the Supreme Being” in the work; filed here as the Cleric.

Leeloo is the Registry's model of the Sealed Light at work, because she brings no spell to the last battle, only a light that must be let through her, and it unmakes the dark it touches.

Leeloo is filed as a Cleric because her decisive act is to relay a closed, restoring light that destroys a corrupting force, through instruments made for it, and only once she consents.

Leeloo is the Registry's model of the Sealed Light at work, because she brings no spell to the last battle, only a light that must be let through her, and it unmakes the dark it touches.

Leeloo comes into the story as the fifth of five elements, rebuilt in a laboratory from a single armoured hand, and when the four stones are set about her in the temple she is meant to be the door through which a light passes. She casts nothing. In a few days she learns all that humankind has done to itself, and in her grief she nearly withholds the light; it is let through only when she is given a reason to love what it would save, and then it pours out of her and turns back the dark that was coming for the world. That is the Cleric's office in its purest form: entrusted, consenting, and no more than the open door.
