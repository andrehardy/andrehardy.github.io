1The SubjectSummary

The Necromancer does not call back the dead, who have gone where no art can follow, but raises the house they left behind and works it from the deep like a puppet on strings.

A Necromancer is an ordinary person who drives dead bodies with a current drawn from the Second Tier, producing crude movement in tissue that no longer holds a person.

2The Account of the StringsHow the power works

The Necromancer is misunderstood by everyone who fears the Necromancer, which is everyone. The dead are not called back. When a life ends, the one who lived it goes up, out of reach of any art practised under the sky, and the Necromancer has never once spoken with a departed soul. What the Necromancer has is the house they left: the bones, the sinews, the cold brain in its case.

That is enough. The deep holds a current that dead flesh will still answer, and the Necromancer knows how to carry it in, by a silver needle at the base of the skull, or by a grey mould sown in the corpse that threads it through like strings. The dead hand closes. The dead knees bend. And the thing gets up and does what it is told, no more and no less, with nothing behind its eyes.

A Necromancer’s wealth is counted in bones. The puppets rot as they walk, so there must always be more; they drop the moment they are not watched, so their master hardly sleeps; and the current that moves them moves through the Necromancer first, and wears the living body down to grey and bone.

Some, it is told, eat of the dead to learn what the dead knew. A few memories linger in the brain for a little while after the tenant has gone, and they can be taken in. It is not conversation. It is rummaging through an empty room.

Two things defeat the Necromancer utterly. The living cannot be worked, for their own tenant holds the strings; to use a living foe, the Necromancer must first make a corpse of them in the ordinary way. And the Sealed Light, wherever it falls, sets the dead flesh back in its first pattern, in which it lay still, and every puppet in the room drops.

Being: a baseline human, worn down by the work. Source: the Second Tier, through dead tissue. Access: Reanimation.

The Necromancer does not command souls. When a person dies, what made them a person is held by the First Tier, beyond the Necromancer's reach. What stays behind is tissue: a nervous system that no longer runs and muscle that no longer moves.

The Necromancer drives that tissue from outside. A current drawn from the Second Tier is carried into the dead nervous system, by a conductor at the base of the skull or by a fungus that grows through the body, and the muscles respond. The result is crude movement under direct control: walking, gripping, lifting, striking. The body has no will and no memory of its own while it moves.

Some traces of memory remain in dead brain tissue for a short time. A Necromancer who consumes or otherwise takes in that tissue may recover fragments of what the person knew. Fiction often presents this as speaking with the dead. It is the reading of residue.

Limits: only dead tissue can be worked, so a living target must be killed first. Bodies continue to decay. Control lasts only while the Necromancer directs it. The work wears down the Necromancer's own body. The Sealed Broadcast stops reanimated bodies within its reach.

3The Instruments of the TableTools

The silver needle

Long and fine, and driven in where the skull meets the spine. Through it the strings are fastened, and the corpse sits up.

A conductor placed at the base of the skull. It carries the current that drives the dead nervous system, and without it, or a substitute, the body cannot be worked.

The grave-mould

A grey bloom scraped from the walls of old tombs and kept in a sealed jar. Sown in a fresh corpse, it threads the dead flesh with strings of its own.

A fungus of the Second Tier that grows through dead tissue and carries the current without a needle. It lets a Necromancer work many bodies at once, at the cost of faster decay.

4Wear, and What It Cannot ReachCosts and blind spots

WearCosts

  1. The deep takes its toll on the one who draws on it. A Necromancer grows thin, grey and cold in the hands, and is old before the age of forty.

    Drawing the current through the body damages the Necromancer's own health over time. Long practice produces visible wasting.

6Before the TrialsTrial results

The Glass upon the TableTrial: bring down a glass

If something lies dead within the walls, it climbs out and pushes the glass over; if not, nothing happens.

The Necromancer has no hold on wine or glass, and none on anything living. But if a mouse died in the wall last winter, the Necromancer finds it, and it comes scrabbling out of the plaster, stiff and wrong in every movement, crosses the table and leans against the glass until it tips. In a room where nothing has died, the Necromancer waits in vain.

The Necromancer can move only dead tissue. If a dead animal is present, for example in a wall cavity, the Necromancer drives it out and uses it to push the glass off. In a room with no remains, the attempt fails.

Liv Moore

iZombie, 2015

Called “zombie” in the work; filed here as the Necromancer.

Liv Moore is the Registry's model Necromancer, because she shows exactly what the dead leave behind: not the person, who is gone, but a few broken memories caught in the flesh, to be taken in by one with the stomach for it.

Liv Moore is filed as a Necromancer because she recovers fragments of a dead person's memory by eating their brain tissue, which is the reading of residue in dead flesh.

Liv Moore is the Registry's model Necromancer, because she shows exactly what the dead leave behind: not the person, who is gone, but a few broken memories caught in the flesh, to be taken in by one with the stomach for it.

She was a young doctor with every prospect, until a night on the water left her pale, cold and hungry for one food only. She takes a post at the city mortuary, where the dead come to her table and there are brains to be had without murder. Each one she eats shows her flashes of its owner's life, unbidden and out of order, and for a while she takes on its owner's habits and humours. With them she helps a detective find who killed the people on her table. She never speaks with the dead; she rummages in what they left. That is the Necromancer's art laid bare, and the Registry knows no plainer picture of it.

In iZombie, medical resident Liv Moore becomes a zombie and must eat human brains to stay herself. She takes a job at the King County Medical Examiner's office in Seattle, where she can eat the brains of the dead. Eating a brain gives her visions of the dead person's memories, triggered by things she sees and hears, and temporarily gives her some of their personality. She uses these fragments to help Detective Clive Babineaux solve murders, presenting herself as a psychic. Reading partial memories out of dead tissue is the part of the Necromancer's access, Reanimation, that deals with residue rather than movement.

Game of Thrones, 2015

Called “the Night King” in the work; filed here as the Necromancer.

The Night King is the Registry's runner-up Necromancer, because he raises the dead in their thousands, empty and obedient, and when he falls every one of them falls with him.

The Night King is filed as a Necromancer because he animates and controls dead bodies, which collapse when he is destroyed, showing that their movement depends entirely on him.

The Night King is the Registry's runner-up Necromancer, because he raises the dead in their thousands, empty and obedient, and when he falls every one of them falls with him.

He was made, not born: a captive bound to a tree, with a blade of black glass driven into his heart by those who hoped to make a weapon of him. What came of it is the master of the dead. In Game of Thrones, the Night King, leader of the White Walkers, raises the dead as wights. In "Hardhome" he raises the newly killed by lifting his arms, and later he raises a slain dragon, Viserion. The wights have no will of their own and act as his army. When Arya Stark kills him in the battle at Winterfell, the wights he raised collapse at once. The dependence of every reanimated body on a single controller, and their collapse when the controller is gone, matches the Necromancer's access, Reanimation.
