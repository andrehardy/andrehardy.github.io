How this almanac is to be read

On reading the almanacHow the Registry is read

  1. Two voicesTwo readings

    Every entry is written twice. The almanac speaks in the manner of an old book of the heavens and the earth; the Cipher sets the same matter down plainly, as an account of how each art is worked. The lantern at the head of each leaf turns the whole leaf from one voice to the other, and the choice is kept from leaf to leaf. Over a passage that carries a lens, a small glass follows the pointer and shows the other voice inside it.

    Every entry has two texts. The almanac text is figurative; the Cipher text states the mechanism plainly: what the power is, where it comes from, and what it costs. The lantern at the head of the page turns every passage to the other text, and the choice is kept from page to page. On passages with a lens, a circle that follows the pointer shows the other text inside it, without turning the page.

  2. The axisThe classification

    The twelve practitioners stand on a single axis, from the Sealed Light at its height, through the Vault of the sky and the Threshold of the horizon, down into the Deep Mantle. Each is filed by where its power comes from, not by what it is called, and its place on the axis is given in figures.

    The twelve archetypes are sorted by the source of their power: the Sealed Tier, the First Tier, the Unaligned and the Second Tier. A practitioner is classified by how the power is obtained (born, studied, bargained, ingested, and so on), never by the name a story gives it. Each has a numeric position on the axis.

  3. The foliosThe archetype entries

    Each practitioner has a folio of its own making, set in the hand and on the ground of its art. Every folio opens with its marks: the Source it draws on, its place on the axis, the Being who practises it and the way in. Then come its Account, its instruments and costs, its answers to the Trials, and the portrayals filed under it.

    Each archetype has an entry set out in its own manner. Every entry gives the source, the axis position, the body types that can practise it and the means of access, then the full account, the instruments and costs, its outcome in each Trial, and the portrayals classified under it.

  4. The registersThe indexes

    Three registers stand behind the folios. The Codex sets what a practitioner is against where the power comes from. The Trials put one task to all twelve and set their answers side by side. The Ledger enters every portrayal, and leads with the misfiled: those a story calls one thing that the Registry files as another.

    Three indexes cross-reference the archetypes. The Codex is a grid of Being by Source. Each Trial compares the outcome of one task across all twelve archetypes. The Ledger lists every portrayal with its verdict, misclassified entries first, and can be filtered by realm, archetype, medium and verdict.

The verdictsVerdict kinds

A verdict is pressed in wax beside every portrayal, bearing the initial of the practitioner it is filed under, and written out in words. It is one of three kinds.Each portrayal carries a wax seal with the initial of its archetype. The verdict is always also given in words, and is one of three kinds.

True to the label
The work and the Registry agree: what the story calls the figure is what it is.The label used in the work matches the classification.
Misfiled
The story calls the figure one thing; the Registry, reading how the power is had, files it as another.The work labels the character as one archetype; the mechanism of its power belongs to another.
An anomaly
A figure who fits no practitioner: an envoy, a being of another order, entered all the same.A character outside the twelve archetypes, recorded with the nearest classification.

Also filed
Every other portrayal entered under the practitioner, by date.Further examples, listed by year.

The signs of the TrialsTrial outcomes

Moved
The task is done.The archetype accomplishes the task.
On terms
The task is done, but only on terms: a price, a condition, a door that must first open.The task succeeds only if a condition is met.
Untouched
The thing is left as it was: the art is not lent for such an errand, or works upon something else.The target is not affected; the power is unavailable for the task or acts on something else.
Shattered
The task is done by ruin: the glass broken, the stranger struck down.The task is achieved destructively, by breaking or harming the target.
Refused
The art turns from the task, for what is asked lies outside its dominion.The power cannot be applied to this target at all, by its nature or its terms.
